# CaptureRecall monetization hypothesis

CaptureRecall should sell the value of a reusable project reference library: a developer finds a saved error or code example, and a team can reuse that context without recreating it. The current free MIT desktop app is the entry point. It does not yet have paid plans, accounts, hosted storage, payments, or team features.

## Recommended offer to validate

Keep local capture, OCR, search, notes, tags, copy, and drag free. Charge for an optional managed service with ongoing costs and team value, rather than changing the current free promise or requiring an account for local work.

| Offer | Proposed value | Price hypothesis in CAD |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free local app | Capture, search, and reuse on one device | $0 |
| Optional Personal service | Cross-device sync and recoverable backup, with explicit opt-in and a documented privacy model | $6–9/month |
| Team service, primary commercial bet | Shared project libraries, search across shared references, access controls, and controlled sharing | $12–18/user/month |
| Deployment and support | Assisted rollout, self-hosted service support where practical, and a support agreement | Quote after discovery |

These prices and features are research hypotheses, not available products or proven willingness to pay. Do not publish a checkout, take advance payment, or promise delivery dates before the offer is defined. Do not advertise encryption, SSO, compliance, unlimited storage, integrations, or uptime guarantees before they exist and have been verified. A proposed Team product is a shared reference workflow, not merely an OCR subscription.

## Why this angle

Paid screenshot tools establish that people pay for workflow convenience. CleanShot currently lists a US$35 one-time Basic license and Pro at US$10/user/month billed annually, adding cloud storage and team controls. Shottr offers one-time purchases. Neither establishes that CaptureRecall users will pay these amounts. The inference is that recurring revenue is more credible when attached to ongoing service and team use than to basic OCR alone.

Sources checked October 10, 2026: [CleanShot pricing](https://cleanshot.com/pricing), [Shottr purchases](https://shottr.cc/purchase.html).

## Validation before building a service

1. Complete five external desktop trials and confirm users can capture, find, and reuse screenshots. Ask about a recent real retrieval problem, rather than whether they like the idea.
2. Interview ten developers or small development/support teams about reference sharing, existing tools, who pays, and what they would replace. Do not send outreach without authorization.
3. Present a clearly labelled Team concept with one shared project library and the CAD $15/user/month midpoint. Ask whether they would start a defined paid pilot, who approves it, and what requirement prevents it. A positive opinion is weaker evidence than an agreed purchase process.
4. Proceed to a scoped service prototype only after three independent teams express concrete paid-pilot intent and native desktop activation is reliable. This is a decision threshold, not a guaranteed forecast.
5. Define storage limits, support costs, data handling, authentication, access revocation, export/deletion, and backup recovery before payment or real customer data. Test actual cost per active user; never infer profitability from price alone.

The next desktop adoption bet is importing existing screenshots, if trials confirm that need. It can strengthen the free entry point without forcing a cloud service. Mac notarization remains a distribution/trust priority. Do not build accounts, billing, sync, and enterprise features all at once before validating which problem buyers will pay to solve.

## Revenue illustration

Twenty teams with five paid seats each at CAD $15/seat/month would produce CAD $1,500 monthly gross subscription revenue, before costs and taxes. This is arithmetic to size the experiment, not a forecast. Track qualified paid-pilot interest and repeat retrieval use, not downloads alone.
